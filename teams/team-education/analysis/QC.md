## What `Vertical Position` means

Treated as **depth below the water surface, in metres**. Evidence: in 268 full casts from June onward, the readings at position < 3 were warmer than those at position > 40 in 268 (median 17.5 °C near position 0 vs 14.3 °C near position 44). Warm water floats, so small positions are near the surface. Each cast starts deep and rises to about 0.9 m. The deepest point stays near 44 m all season while the reservoir level changes, which fits a profiler hung from a float, measuring down from the surface. **Denver Water should confirm this**, and say where the sonde sits relative to the dam and the Foothills intake.

## `Conductivity` is not temperature-compensated

Within each full cast, conductivity tracks temperature almost exactly (median correlation 0.98). After the standard linear correction to 25 °C (coefficient 0.0191 per °C, general knowledge), the correlation is 0.00 and the column matches the specific conductance reported by the USGS gage at the reservoir inflow (see `analysis/storm_trace.py`). So the logged value is raw conductivity at water temperature, and its apparent layering is mostly temperature. The clean file adds `spcond_us_cm`, the compensated value; all findings use it. Denver Water should confirm the sonde's setting.

The spike rule is a Hampel filter: each value is compared to the median of up to 2 neighbours either side in depth order within its own cast. On a smooth or step-shaped profile (a thermocline) that median tracks the profile, so only isolated departures are flagged.

## Sensor servicing: not corrected, but it limits interpretation

On these days the median across all depths jumped from the previous observed day, by more than 100 mV of ORP or 0.7 ug/L of chlorophyll: Apr 28, May 04, May 14, May 18, Jun 16, Jul 20, Aug 18. A change that appears at every depth on the same day is the instrument, most likely cleaning, recalibration or redeployment (several follow a gap in the record, when the sonde was presumably out of the water), not the water. The clean file carries a `segment` number that increases at each event, so analysis can compare within a segment.

Consequences: ORP is not comparable across segments and is left out of the findings. Chlorophyll sits at a flat negative floor in some segments, which reads as an offset, not an absence of algae, so chlorophyll and phycocyanin are only compared within a segment. Small negative chlorophyll values are kept as reported.

- The units of `Phycocyanin` (no unit in the header; probably RFU or ug/L).
